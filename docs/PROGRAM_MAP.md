## 4. Three blindness levels

The program must keep three failure depths separate:

```text
policy blindness
!=
retention blindness
!=
architectural blindness
```

They differ in where potentially corrective information is lost.

### Policy blindness

The relevant observation/intervention channel exists, but the current model drives acquisition away from it.

```text
available channel
+
model-conditioned acquisition
-> evidence under-sampled or avoided
```

Informal compression:

```text
"the learner could look there, but its current policy says not to"
```

BL×CC-001 partially engages this depth: the current map changes which ordinary intervention is chosen and therefore changes the distribution of corrective evidence. Crucially, the wrong-map-selected probe remains informative and the alternative probe retains a nonzero floor.

### Retention blindness

The learner acquires an observation, but the current model or memory process prevents it from surviving into future reasoning or revision.

```text
observation acquired
-> model-conditioned retention/compression
-> potentially corrective evidence deleted or inaccessible
```

Informal compression:

```text
"the learner looked there, but did not keep what mattered"
```

No completed BL/BL×CC assay establishes retention-stage corrigibility.

### Architectural blindness

The current system lacks the representational, observational, or interventional capacity needed to instantiate the relevant distinction at all.

```text
needed distinction/channel
not expressible or reachable by current architecture
```

Informal compression:

```text
"there is no available way, in the current architecture, to form or access the needed distinction"
```

Architectural blindness is stronger than policy or retention blindness. Random exploration or protected retention cannot create a missing sensor, action, representation, or hypothesis dimension.

A missing physical observation/intervention channel is one form of architectural blindness; a missing representational distinction is another. They should not be silently collapsed if a future assay requires separating them.

Durable principle:

> **Randomness can protect against endogenous selection, but cannot create a missing observation, intervention, or representational channel.**
